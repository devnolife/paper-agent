## copilot:claude-haiku-4.5 (requested copilot:claude-haiku-4.5, 43.9 s)

**Summary.** The paper identifies and addresses silent model substitution in vendor LLM SDKs, where requests for one model are served by another without notification. The authors build paper-agent, which logs served models via usage events, routes tasks by role to different models, and wraps all outputs with deterministic validators. On a 146-unit IEEE paper translation to Indonesian, a three-model pipeline maintains structural integrity but shows no mean quality improvement over a single model, with benefits appearing primarily in error catching.

**Strengths**
- Identifies a genuine and consequential problem for scientific reproducibility (silent model substitution in vendor SDKs)
- Proposes a practical, immediately actionable solution (reading served model from usage events) with clear recommendations
- Comprehensive system design integrating multiple components (role-based routing, structure-safe masking, validators)
- Valuable per-unit error analysis distinguishing which errors are caught by models vs. validators vs. human inspection
- Interesting finding that deterministic validators catch structural errors (moved placeholders, dropped placeholders, echoed outputs) that no model detected
- Honest acknowledgment of limitations, particularly the single-run nature and lack of human evaluation
- Code and per-unit metrics promised for release
- Well-contextualized literature review connecting to compound systems and LLM-as-judge limitations

**Overclaims**
- Three error classes were detected by no model, including the judge, but caught by validators — One error (echoed back-translation) was produced by the QA model itself, not missed by models; it's not evidence that models generally fail to detect this class. The other two (moved/dropped placeholders) are specific to one model's failure, not representative of general model weaknesses.
- The pipeline is the only configuration with full structural integrity — Table 2 shows all three configurations achieved 146/146 structural integrity (though the multi and single-Haiku never fell back to English while single-gemma3 left 2 paragraphs untranslated, still only 2/146 failures).

**Missing**
- Ablation study separating the contributions of validators, multi-model routing, and post-editing roles
- Human evaluation of translation quality by bilingual domain experts, particularly for semantic errors claimed to be caught
- Multiple runs per configuration to account for LLM output variability and enable statistical significance testing
- Analysis of glossary compliance differences (99.7% vs 99.0% vs 97.9%) - what linguistic phenomena explain these?
- Comparison of validators' error detection with other automatic quality estimation methods (e.g., reference-based metrics if available)
- Testing of Experiment A across additional vendor SDKs and account tiers to establish generalizability beyond Copilot
- Per-content-type analysis of which units (tables, headings, paragraphs, citations) benefit most from review
- Baseline comparison with existing MT evaluation frameworks or simpler multi-model approaches

## ollama:gemma3:27b (requested ollama:gemma3:27b, 35.3 s)

**Summary.** This paper investigates silent model substitution in LLM-based research agents, presenting 'paper-agent' – an open-source tool for scientific paper processing. The authors demonstrate that the model name reported by some SDKs may not reflect the actual model used, and propose logging the served model for provenance. They compare a multi-model pipeline with single-model configurations for LaTeX translation, finding that while the pipeline doesn't improve average quality, it excels at error detection through deterministic validation.

**Strengths**
- The identification of silent model substitution is a significant practical issue for reproducibility.
- The emphasis on deterministic validation as a complementary approach to LLM-based review is valuable.
- The detailed experimental setup and release of code and data enhance reproducibility.
- The system design, with clear separation of roles and provenance tracking, is well-structured.
- The error analysis provides insightful observations about the types of errors different components can catch.

**Overclaims**
- The pipeline 'corrected semantic errors that no metric showed'. — While the authors identify errors caught by the post-editor, they haven't demonstrated that these errors would have gone completely undetected by other means (e.g., human review). The metrics used may simply be insensitive to these specific errors, not that they are entirely invisible.
- Validators caught three error classes that no model, including the judge, detected. — The claim is based on the specific errors found in this experiment. It's possible that other models, with different prompts or training data, could detect these errors.

**Missing**
- A comparison with other existing research agents or automated paper processing tools.
- A more thorough analysis of the computational cost of the validation process.
- A discussion of the limitations of using SBERT embeddings for cross-lingual similarity assessment.
- A more detailed explanation of the glossary creation process and its potential biases.
